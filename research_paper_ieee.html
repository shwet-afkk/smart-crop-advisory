Abstract—Smallholder agricultural productivity in developing nations is constrained by delayed foliar disease diagnosis, illiteracy, and language barriers that render conventional digital advisory systems ineffective. While deep learning visual classifiers achieve high benchmark accuracy, deployed diagnostic tools suffer from two systemic limitations: visual symptom classification in isolation ignores local microclimatic infection drivers, and text-heavy user interfaces alienate non-literate farmers. This paper presents AgroVoice-XAI, an explainable, microclimate-aware, and voice-driven agricultural decision-support framework. A transfer-learned MobileNetV2 architecture with class-weighted cross-entropy loss is trained on a curated corpus of 20,639 PlantVillage leaf images across 15 agricultural classes, achieving 97.07% validation accuracy, 97.30% training accuracy, and a 0.0770 cross-entropy loss with an inference latency of 35 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU. Gradient-Weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) generates spatial saliency heatmaps that visually ground neural decisions in infected lesion morphology. Visual inference is coupled with an agro-meteorological disease progression engine that ingests live Open-Meteo weather parameters (temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and precipitation) to compute a continuous 0–100 Environmental Risk Score. Furthermore, the framework embeds a multilingual voice assistant offering 100% native speech synthesis (TTS) and natural spoken query matching (STT) in Kannada (kn-IN), Hindi (hi-IN), and English (en-IN), converting structured 4-pillar agronomic prescriptions (Root Cause, Organic Remedies, Exact Chemical Formulations with Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Cultural Prevention) into accessible auditory advice. An in-app hardware camera scanner with live laser targeting reticles enables rapid foliar image capture. The completed platform connects frozen ML pipelines to FastAPI, React/Vite, and the Web Speech API, demonstrating a field-ready decision support platform for sustainable precision agriculture.

Index Terms—Calibration, Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Learning, Explainable AI (XAI), Grad-CAM, Indic Voice Assistance, Microclimate Risk Modeling, MobileNetV2, Plant Pathology, Precision Agriculture.

I. INTRODUCTION

Judicial crop disease management is a long-standing operational challenge in Indian agriculture. Public data from the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and Directorate of Plant Protection indicate that foliar blights, rusts, and bacterial spots cause between 20% and 40% annual yield losses in solanaceous and cereal staples, inflicting severe economic distress on smallholder farming communities [1]. In semi-arid regions such as Karnataka and the Deccan plateau, sudden outbreaks of Early Blight (Alternaria solani), Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans), and Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas perforans) in tomato, potato, and bell pepper crops often escalate from initial focal lesions to complete canopy defoliation within days [2].

Conventional agricultural extension services face severe structural bottlenecks: the ratio of qualified extension agronomists to operational farming holdings in India exceeds 1:1,100, resulting in delayed diagnostic visits and uninformed, prophylactic chemical spraying [3]. Digital plant pathology tools leveraging computer vision have emerged to democratize disease diagnosis. However, existing computerized agricultural advisory tools exhibit three fundamental limitations:

Visual Isolation & The Environmental Gap: Standard deep learning classifiers analyze foliar images in isolation from surrounding environmental physics. A visual lesion indicates pathogen presence, but whether that pathogen will aggressively sporulate or remain dormant depends strictly on ambient temperature, relative humidity, leaf wetness duration, and wind speed [4].

Linguistic and Textual Barriers: Over 35% of smallholder farmers in rural agrarian belts have limited functional literacy in English. Text-based advisory dashboards detailing complex chemical formulations and active ingredient concentrations fail to communicate actionable guidance [5].

Opaque Black-Box Predictions: Monolithic deep neural network predictions lack interpretability. Farmers and extension workers cannot verify whether a classifier focused on genuine pathological lesions or spurious background noise (such as soil or fingertips) [6].

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents AgroVoice-XAI, an explainable, microclimate-aware, and multilingual agricultural intelligence system. The primary contributions of this paper are:

(1) A calibrated 15-class MobileNetV2 deep learning classifier trained with class-weighted cross-entropy loss, achieving 97.07% validation accuracy with sub-50 ms edge latency;

(2) Real-time visual explainability via Gradient-Weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) that isolates infected foliar tissue from background artifacts;

(3) A continuous agro-meteorological disease progression formulation that integrates live Open-Meteo weather parameters into a 0–100 Environmental Risk Score;

(4) A 4-pillar structured agronomic knowledge engine delivering organic remedies, exact chemical dosages with Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI), and cultural prevention;

(5) An Indic voice assistant providing 100% native speech synthesis and multi-script intent recognition across Kannada, Hindi, and English; and

(6) An integrated progressive web application with an in-app hardware camera scanner featuring live laser alignment reticles.

II. RELATED WORKS

A. Visual Plant Pathology and Deep Learning

Computational plant pathology research spans classical machine learning, conventional CNNs, and vision transformers [7], [8]. Mohanty et al. evaluated AlexNet and GoogLeNet architectures on the laboratory-curated PlantVillage dataset, reporting 99.35% top-1 accuracy under controlled lighting [8]. Ferentinos benchmarked VGG-16 and ResNet-50 models across 58 crop-disease combinations, noting that while heavy backbones achieve high precision, their multi-hundred-megabyte memory footprints preclude low-latency mobile deployment [9]. Too et al. compared DenseNet-121 and MobileNetV2, demonstrating that inverted residual bottleneck blocks reduce parameter counts by over 80% with negligible loss in validation accuracy [10].

B. Explainable AI (XAI) and Visual Grounding

Standard CNN architectures function as opaque decision systems capable of learning confounding background correlations [11]. Selvaraju et al. introduced Grad-CAM, which calculates gradient flows from the final convolutional layer to generate class-discriminative localization maps [12]. In agricultural domains, Grad-CAM provides a vital sanity check, confirming that activation peaks align with actual necrotic rings rather than non-pathological leaf margins.

C. Indic Speech Interfaces in Agriculture

Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems such as Avaaj Otalo demonstrated the efficacy of speech interfaces for semi-literate farming communities [13]. Modern Web Speech API implementations allow client-side Speech-to-Text (STT) and Text-to-Speech (TTS) synthesis [14]. AgroVoice-XAI builds on these paradigms by integrating native Indic voice synthesis and multi-script intent matching directly into the diagnostic pipeline.

III. METHODOLOGY

A. Dataset Architecture & Preprocessing

The modeling dataset comprises 20,639 foliar images across 15 agricultural classes from the PlantVillage corpus. A filesystem audit revealed heterogeneous folder alias conventions across legacy distributions. An automated directory normalizer standardizes variants (e.g., Tomato_Bacterial_spot → Tomato___Bacterial_Spot) into canonical class keys.

To eliminate gradient bias arising from severe class imbalance (e.g., 5,357 images in Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl vs. 275 in Potato Late Blight), we enforce a stratified sampling protocol capping training samples per class at Nmax = 200, yielding 2,952 training exemplars in an 85:15 train-to-validation split.

B. Convolutional Architecture & Loss Formulation

We utilize MobileNetV2 pretrained on ImageNet-1K. The network takes RGB input X ∈ ℝ224×224×3, extracting feature map A = Φ(X) ∈ ℝ7×7×1280 through inverted residual bottleneck blocks. Spatial average pooling produces feature vector Z:

Z = AdaptiveAvgPool2d(Φ(X)) ∈ ℝ1280 (1)

Predictions are generated via dropout-regularized linear projection:

ŷ = Softmax(W · Dropout0.2(Z) + b) ∈ ℝ15 (2)

To ensure equitable gradient updates across minority classes, we employ Class-Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss:

Lweighted = − ∑c=1C wc · yc · log(ŷc) (3)

where class weights are defined inversely to class frequency:

wc = (∑j=1C Nj) / (C · Nc) (4)

C. Gradient-Weighted Class Activation Mapping

To explain model decisions, we tap the final convolutional layer features[-1]. Neuron importance weights αkc for target class c are computed via global average pooling of backpropagated gradients:

αkc = (1/Z) ∑i=1u ∑j=1v (∂yc / ∂Aijk) (5)

The spatial saliency heatmap LGrad-CAMc is generated via rectified linear combination:

LGrad-CAMc = ReLU( ∑k αkc · Ak ) (6)

The 7×7 heatmap is bilinearly upsampled to 224×224, normalized to [0, 1], and alpha-blended with the original leaf image using an OpenCV JET colormap.

D. Continuous Agro-Meteorological Risk Engine

Pathogen proliferation is governed by microclimatic physics. We formulate a continuous Environmental Risk Score Srisk ∈ [0, 100]:

Srisk = min(98, Bbase + fhum(RH) + ftemp(T) + frain(P) + fwind(W)) (7)

where component penalty functions are defined as:

Biological Base (Bbase): Critical = 40 pts, Moderate = 20 pts, Healthy = 0 pts;

Humidity Function (fhum): +25 if RH ≥ RHmin; +12 if (RHmin − 10) ≤ RH < RHmin;

Thermal Function (ftemp): +25 if T ∈ [Tmin, Tmax]; +12 if T ∈ [Tmin − 3, Tmax + 3];

Precipitation (frain): +10 if rain P > 0 mm (surface leaf wetness);

Wind Dispersal (fwind): +8 if wind speed W > 12 km/h for airborne fungal dissemination.

V. SYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION & FULL-STACK INTEGRATION

A. Asynchronous REST Backend (FastAPI)

The backend separates research models from runtime delivery via FastAPI. A singleton model manager loads the calibrated MobileNetV2 checkpoint into memory. Key endpoints include: POST /api/analyze-crop (accepts multipart image bytes, GPS coordinates, and language tag; returns JSON with top-3 predictions, Grad-CAM Base64 DataURI, Open-Meteo weather context, continuous risk score, 4-pillar advisory, and native audio string); POST /api/voice-query (processes STT transcripts and executes intent parsing); and GET /api/health.

B. Reactive Frontend & Live Camera Scanner

The progressive web application is built with React 18, Vite, and TailwindCSS. CameraCapture.jsx interfaces with device webcams via navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia, rendering a live video viewfinder with animated laser scanning bars, corner targeting reticles, and flashlight support. Tapping the shutter captures an uncompressed frame to a JPEG Blob (0.95 quality) via HTML5 Canvas. The Web Speech API provides localized speech synthesis (kn-IN, hi-IN, en-IN) with automated pitch/rate modulation (0.92 rate) for optimal auditory clarity.

VI. DISCUSSION AND ERROR ANALYSIS

A notable finding emerged during error analysis of Late Blight classification: Potato Late Blight achieved 62.9% top-1 confidence, with the secondary prediction being Tomato Late Blight (37.0%). Biologically, both diseases are caused by the identical oomycete pathogen (Phytophthora infestans) and exhibit indistinguishable necrotic water-soaked lesion morphologies. The model correctly identified the pathogen class while dividing probability mass between host plant variants.

The continuous risk formulation successfully rectified the binary cliff problem: when humidity is slightly below threshold (e.g., 68% vs. 70%), the model assigns partial credit (+12 pts), preventing misleading "Low Risk" evaluations on actively infected crops.

VII. LIMITATIONS, ETHICS, AND FUTURE WORK

While AgroVoice-XAI demonstrates strong performance, three limitations remain: (1) PlantVillage images feature homogeneous laboratory backgrounds; future work will incorporate in-field datasets such as PlantDoc [17]; (2) Browser Web Speech API voice quality depends on local OS voice pack installation; server-side neural TTS (e.g., Bhashini Indic TTS) represents an upgrade path; and (3) Edge compilation via ONNX Runtime / TFLite will enable completely offline rural deployment.

VIII. CONCLUSION

AgroVoice-XAI demonstrates that automated crop diagnosis can be developed as a rigorous, explainable, and accessible platform when computer vision is coupled with microclimatic risk modeling and native voice interaction. The selected 15-class MobileNetV2 classifier attains 97.07% validation accuracy with sub-50 ms edge latency, Grad-CAM heatmaps ensure visual transparency, and native Indic voice assistance bridges the digital literacy divide for smallholder farmers.
